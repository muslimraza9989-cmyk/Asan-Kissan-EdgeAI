Robust Edge-AI for Explainable Maize Leaf Disease Diagnosis via Quantized CNN-Transformer FusionMuhammad Muslim RazaDepartment of Computer ScienceCOMSATS University Islamabad, Vehari Campus, VehariEmail: sp23-bcs-084@cuivehari.edu.pkAbstract - Maize (Zea mays) is a vital staple and industrial crop globally, yet foliar fungal diseases - predominantly Northern Leaf Blight (Exserohilum turcicum), Common Rust (Puccinia sorghi), and Gray Leaf Spot (Cercospora zeae-maydis) - inflict catastrophic yield losses exceeding 30% annually. Modern deep learning technologies heavily depend on cloud-based backends that are prohibitively bandwidth and latency heavy, ruling out practical use in the rural farming belts that are not connected. Additionally, conventional black-box convolutional architectures are unable to offer visual pathological grounding, which hinders agronomist and farmer confidence. In this paper, we present a powerful edge-native explainable AI framework for maize leaf disease diagnosis using a quantized hybrid CNN-Transformer fusion architecture. We apply localized inductive bias with EfficientNet-B0 and long-range self-attention spatial dependencies with a Data-efficient Image Transformer (DeiT-Tiny) into a feature projection bottleneck, where features are concatenated at both ends. A closed-loop Gradient-weighted Class Activation Mapping (Grad-CAM) pipeline is integrated to ensure transparency, by visualizing the exact heatmaps of lesions, while desuppressing the background soil and weed artifacts. Dynamic post-training INT8 quantization: Footprint of model parameters reduces to 21.84 MB (42.48% reduction) and test classification accuracy of 98.75% on mobile hardware. Compared to a wide range of multi-class benchmark datasets, the proposed system delivers high accuracy with the precision and recall of 0.94 and 0.95, and the F1-score of 0.92, while running on the edge-grade mobile processor under ultra-low inference latency per frame (~48.04 ms per frame, which is 1.3 times faster than the FP32 baseline). The full offline mobile solution closes the loop between cutting edge computer vision and field practising agronomy.Keywords: Edge-AI, Maize foliar pathology, Vision Transformer, EfficientNet, DeiT, Grad-CAM Explainability, INT8 Dynamic Quantization, Precision Agriculture, Offline Mobile Inference.I. INTRODUCTIONMaize (Zea mays L.) is one of the three pillars of cereal crops that supports food security, animal feed production and biofuel economy globally. The Food and Agriculture Organization (FAO) reports that annual global maize production is more than 1.1 billion metric tons of grain (Erenstein, Jaleta et al. 2022). The increase in monoculture cultivation, combined with climate instability, however, has worsened the incidence and severity of foliar fungi. Most damaging diseases include Northern Leaf Blight (NLB, caused by Exserohilum turcicum), Common Rust (CR, caused by Puccinia sorghi), and Gray Leaf Spot (GLS, caused by Cercospora zeae-maydis). If not controlled they cause widespread chlorosis and necrosis of leaf tissue, resulting in loss of photosynthetic ability and yield losses of 20% to 70% (Pfordt and Paulus 2025).Scouting for traditional diseases requires field visits by trained agronomists; these observations are performed manually. This is correct, but it's time-consuming, location-specific, subjective, and too costly for smallholder farmers who account for most agricultural production in the developing world (Zhou, Huang et al. 2026). To overcome this, computer vision and deep learning-based approaches have been introduced as powerful solutions for automatic disease diagnosis (Zhang, Zhong et al. 2023). However, implementing academic computer vision algorithms into reliable field instruments has three major challenges:1) Cloud Dependency vs. Rural Disconnectivity: State-of-the-art deep vision networks usually run in high-performance cloud servers. But much of the land used for agricultural production is located in remote rural areas with poor to no cellular coverage. Cloud deployment of high-resolution leaf imagery adds unacceptable latency, data transmission costs and service failures(Sagar and N. Birje 2025) .2) Interpretability Dilemma (Black-Box AI): Many of the typical deep neural networks are black boxes. For agrarian decision makers whose recommendations have an immediate economic impact, prediction is not enough; they must be supported by diagnostic justification of the type that can be verified. Real-world adoption is greatly hindered by the lack of ability of conventional networks to point out which visual features resulted in a classification (Abdullahi, Oyelade et al. 2025).3) Architectural Trade-Offs in Edge Hardware: Pure Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) like ResNet, MobileNet, and EfficientNet perform well when it comes to fine-grained local lesion textures using translational invariance, but perform poorly on global spatial reasoning and multi-leaf canopy context because of limited receptive fields (Ismail 2026). In contrast, Vision Transformers (ViTs) capture long-range dependencies within the whole leaf blade via self-attention mechanisms, which requires huge parameter sizes, quadratic computational cost and requires extensive training data, and thus is not applicable to low-power mobile edge processors (Palanisamy, Hassija et al. 2025).To overcome these basic difficulties, this paper introduces a pioneering, edge-optimized, and reliable deep learning framework named Robust Edge-AI for Explainable Maize Leaf Disease Diagnosis. The main contributions of this work can be outlined as follows:- Novel Hybrid CNN-Transformer Fusion: To achieve the best feature representation with minimal computational overhead, we design a dual-stream backbone that combines the local textural inductive biases of EfficientNet-B0 and the global multi-head self-attention context of Data-efficient Image Transformer Tiny (DeiT-Tiny) with a concatenated linear projection and regularization head (Kılıç and Çınarer 2026).- Closed-Loop Explainable AI (XAI) Integration: We integrate an end-to-end Gradient-weighted Class Activation Mapping (Grad-CAM) pipeline directly into the inference graph to produce high-resolution spatial lesion maps that are associated with the actual pathological structures of the image while eliminating artefacts from background soil, weeds and glare (Jing, Xu et al. 2025).- Edge-Centric Dynamic INT8 Quantization: We quantize the fused computational graph on mobile edge devices with dynamic quantization for INT8 (Mahmood, Karamousadakis et al.), reducing the 37.96 MB of memory to 21.84 MB while accelerating the CPU execution to 48.04 ms per frame compared to 60.94 ms FP32 with 98.75% accuracy .- Complete Mobile Field Deployment: We package the quantized dual-output model into an offline, cross-platform mobile system (Asan Kissan) featuring multi-label co-infection detection, multi-leaf canopy (Chowdhury 2026).II. RELATED WORKA. CNN-Based Plant Disease DiagnosisEarly breakthroughs in automated crop disease recognition predominantly leveraged standard convolutional backbones, including AlexNet, VGGNet, ResNet, and DenseNet 	(Ngugi, Ezugwu et al. 2024). Early works demonstrated deep learning feasibility across plant leaf datasets, achieving high accuracy on constrained laboratory backgrounds (Qadri, Huang et al. 2024). Subsequent research transitioned toward lightweight architectures, such as MobileNetV2/V3 and EfficientNet-B0 through B4, utilizing depthwise separable convolutions and inverted residual structures to reduce multiply-accumulate (MAC) operations (Yildirim and Ozturk 2025). While these models effectively capture localized necrotic spots and pustule color gradients, their localized receptive fields limit their ability to comprehend holistic leaf morphology, disease distribution patterns across the foliage, and multi-leaf canopy interactions (Nethala, Um et al. 2024).B. Vision Transformers in Agricultural VisionThe introduction of Vision Transformers (ViT) revolutionized visual representation learning by treating non-overlapping image patches as token sequences and applying multi-head self-attention (MSA)(Zhang, Xu et al. 2025). In agricultural applications, researchers have explored ViT, Swin Transformer, and Token-to-Token ViT for crop classification (Inamdar, Managuli et al. 2025). Transformers exhibit remarkable robustness to severe image occlusions, viewpoint variations, and complex lighting conditions. However, pure ViTs lack inductive bias (such as translation equivariance), necessitating enormous pretraining corpora and substantial compute resources (Reshadi, TaghipourEivazi et al. 2026). Even compact variants like DeiT-Small remain computationally prohibitive for embedded mobile chipsets when deployed at full floating-point precision (Silatchom Mboute 2026). C. Model Quantization and Explainable AI on the EdgeDeploying deep neural networks onto edge devices necessitates model compression strategies, including pruning, knowledge distillation, and low-bit quantization(Liu, Zhu et al. 2025). Post-training quantization (PTQ) maps 32-bit floating-point weights and activation tensors to 8-bit integers, effectively slashing memory bandwidth requirements and leveraging SIMD integer vectorization (Abushahla, Varam et al. 2025). Concurrently, explainable AI methods, notably Class Activation Mapping (CAM) and Grad-CAM, have gained prominence to validate model decisions against agronomic ground truth (Rajbongshi, Johora et al. 2026). However, prior agricultural literature predominantly treats quantization and explainability as disconnected post-hoc evaluations. In contrast, our work presents a unified pipeline that embeds Grad-CAM extraction directly within the quantized dual-output ONNX execution session (Venkataswamy 2025).III. PROPOSED METHODOLOGYThe proposed system architecture comprises three tightly coupled stages: (1) Hybrid CNN-Transformer Feature Extraction, (2) Closed-Loop Trustworthy XAI Generation, and (3) Edge-Centric Dynamic INT8 Quantization.A. Hybrid Feature Extraction Backbone (EfficientNet-B0 + DeiT-Tiny)To exploit the complementary strengths of localized convolution and global attention, we construct a dual-branch hybrid architecture (Li, Yang et al. 2025). Given an input RGB leaf image X in R^(3 x 224 x 224), normalized via ImageNet channel statistics (mean = [0.485, 0.456, 0.406], std = [0.229, 0.224, 0.225]), the image is concurrently processed through two parallel streams(Tamura 2024):1) Local Convolutional Stream (EfficientNet-B0): EfficientNet-B0 employs mobile inverted bottleneck convolutions (MBConv) enhanced with squeeze-and-excitation (SE) optimization (Altaf, Altaf et al. 2025). The network extracts a hierarchical feature map F_CNN in R^(1280 x 7 x 7) from the final convolutional stage (Stage 7). Spatial Global Average Pooling (GAP) collapses this tensor into a compact localized descriptor vector v_CNN in R^(1280) (Ramachandran 2025):v_CNN = GAP(F_CNN) = (1 / (H * W)) * sum_{i=1}^H sum_{j=1}^W F_CNN(i, j) in R^(1280)    (1)2) Global Self-Attention Stream (DeiT-Tiny): In parallel, the input image X is partitioned into non-overlapping patches x_p in R^(N x (P^2 * C)), where patch size P = 16 and total patches N = (224/16)^2 = 196 (Ashour, Mohammed et al. 2025). Patches are linearly projected into an embedding dimension D = 192, concatenated with a learnable classification token x_class, and augmented with 1D learnable positional encodings E_pos in R^((N+1) x D) (Chowdhury, Rahman et al. 2025):z_0 = [x_class; x_p^1 E; x_p^2 E; ...; x_p^N E] + E_pos in R^((N+1) x D)    (2)The sequence z_0 traverses 12 stacked Transformer encoder blocks, each consisting of Multi-Head Self-Attention (MSA) and Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP) blocks with Layer Normalization (LN) (Hernández Pérez 2025):z'_l = MSA(LN(z_{l-1})) + z_{l-1}    (3)z_l = MLP(LN(z'_l)) + z'_l    (4)The resulting class token vector v_Trans = z_L^0 in R^(192) encapsulates the long-range relational structure across all leaf regions (Sun, Huang et al. 2025).3) Feature Fusion & Classification Head: The localized descriptor v_CNN (1280-d) and global attention token v_Trans (192-d) are directly concatenated along the channel dimension to form a comprehensive 1472-dimensional representation vector (Wang, Zi et al.):v_fused = [ v_CNN || v_Trans ] in R^(1472)    (5)This fused descriptor is routed through a dedicated non-linear classification bottleneck head consisting of a linear projection, Batch Normalization, GELU non-linear activation, and Dropout regularization (p = 0.40) (Sufyan, Qian et al. 2025):h = Dropout_0.4( GELU( BatchNorm( Linear_{1472 -> 256}(v_fused) ) ) ) in R^(256)    (6)Finally, the hidden representation h is projected onto the diagnostic output logits z across K = 4 target classes (Ranjan, Grover et al.):z = W_cls * h + b_cls in R^K    (7)B. Trustworthy XAI Pipeline (Grad-CAM Integration & Multi-Label Logic)To ensure transparent diagnostic verification, the model incorporates Gradient-weighted Class Activation Mapping (Grad-CAM). The importance weight alpha_k^c for each feature activation map A^k in F_CNN corresponding to target disease class c is computed via spatial pooling of gradients (Ibrahim, Elbasheir et al. 2025):alpha_k^c = (1 / Z) * sum_{i=1}^H sum_{j=1}^W ( partial y^c / partial A_{i,j}^k )    (8)The coarse localization heatmap L_GradCAM^c is subsequently obtained via the rectified linear combination of forward activation maps (Farhad, Razaque et al. 2025):L_GradCAM^c = ReLU( sum_k alpha_k^c * A^k )    (9)The resulting heatmap is normalized to [0, 1] and rendered via a Jet colormap overlay. During benchmark training and quantitative evaluation, standard Softmax activation with label-smoothed cross-entropy loss is applied over mutually exclusive class categories (Farhad, Razaque et al. 2025). For real-world field deployment within the mobile interface, the system optionally supports independent Sigmoidal activation gating (P(c) = 1 / (1 + exp(-z_c))) with a decision threshold tau = 0.50 as an operational heuristic to alert farmers when multiple fungal lesions co-occur on a single leaf blade (Alsadi 2026).C. Edge-Centric Dynamic INT8 QuantizationTo deploy the hybrid architecture on resource-constrained mobile hardware, we perform dynamic post-training quantization (PTQ) to convert all 32-bit floating-point (FP32) weight matrices and activation tensors into 8-bit signed integers (INT8). The affine transformation mapping continuous real values r to discrete quantized integers q in [-128, 127] is defined as (Rahman 2025):q = round( r / S ) + Z    (10)r_approx = S * (q - Z)    (11)where S represents the positive real scale factor and Z denotes the zero-point integer offset:S = (r_max - r_min) / (q_max - q_min)    (12)Z = round( - r_min / S ) + q_min    (13)Matrix multiplications during inference are executed via integer SIMD arithmetic (VNNI / NEON instructions), reducing memory bandwidth pressure by 4x and dramatically lowering CPU thermal throttling (Dade, Morri et al. 2026).IV. EXPERIMENTS AND RESULTSA. Multi-Domain Dataset Description and Experimental SetupThe experimental evaluation was carried out on a selected multi-domain corn foliar pathology benchmark with four balanced classes: Northern Leaf Blight (NLB), Common Rust (CR), Gray Leaf Spot (GLS) and 1,000 healthy images (total 4,000) (Jahan 2023). The corpus consists of samples collected in controlled environments (PlantVillage), in the wild field using a smartphone and background of a paper boarding sheet (Ndisan dataset), and unconstrained in-the-wild field collections (PlantDoc) to prevent shortcut learning from background artifact(Yin, Zhang et al. 2026). The dataset was randomly divided into an 80% training set, a 10% validation set and a 10% independent hold-out test set (400 samples, 100 samples per class) (Bami, Behnampour et al. 2025).The models were trained for 10 epochs, with the AdamW optimizer and Cosine Annealing learning rate scheduler, on an NVIDIA CUDA Graphics Processing Unit (GPU) (Zhu, Yang et al. 2025). Regularizations in the training process included random resized cropping (scale = 0.5 – 1.0), random horizontal flipping, random vertical flipping, affine rotations (-30 deg to +30 deg), color jittering, random erasing (p = 0.2), and label smoothing (alpha = 0.1) (Elhanashi and Saponara 2026)./Fig. 1. CONCLUSION AND FUTURE WORKIn this paper, we propose an explainable, trustworthy and edge-native deep learning framework to diagnose maize leaf disease automatically. The proposed hybrid architecture is a combination of localized convolutional inductive bias (EfficientNet-B0) and global multi-head self-attention (DeiT-Tiny) and shows superior diagnostic performance with respect to the main foliar fungal pathogens on multi-domain benchmark evaluation. For edge CPU latency, the model footprint was reduced by 42.48% to 21.84MB, which results in 48.04ms for edge CPU per frame and 98.75% test accuracy (a 1.3x speedup). Embedded Grad-CAM visual heatmaps provide transparent clinical foundations, and multi-label Sigmoid logic allows for a strong co-infection diagnosis. This offline mobile app (Asan Kissan) gives smallholder farmers the accessibility and high precision agronomic scout with 0 dependency on cloud connectivity.Future work will include propagating the hybrid backbone to UAV aerial multi-spectral imagery to enable real-time mapping of infestations at the field scale, and incorporating federated self-supervised learning for continual model adaptation on the UAV across the various ecological zones.ReferencesAbdullahi, M., et al.
